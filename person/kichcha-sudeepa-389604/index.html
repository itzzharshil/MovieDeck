**Sudeep Sanjeev** (born 2 September 1973), universally and affectionately known as **Kiccha Sudeep** or **Sudeepa**, is a highly acclaimed Indian actor, film director, producer, screenwriter, television presenter, and playback singer who predominantly works in Kannada cinema. With a massive pan-Indian appeal and a prolific career spanning over two decades, he has established himself as one of the most prominent, versatile, and highest-paid superstars in the South Indian film industry.

Born in Shimoga, Karnataka, to Sanjeev Manjappa and Saroja, Sudeep pursued a Bachelor's degree in Industrial and Production Engineering from Dayananda Sagar College of Engineering in Bengaluru. Driven by a deep passion for the performing arts, he later honed his craft at the renowned Roshan Taneja School of Acting in Mumbai. He made his feature film debut in 1997 with *Thayavva*, but first gained significant recognition for his lead role in Sunil Kumar Desai's romantic drama *Sparsha* (2000).

His major cinematic breakthrough and career-defining moment arrived with the 2001 romantic action drama *Huchcha*. His intense and deeply moving portrayal of a college student who loses his sanity earned him the enduring moniker "Kiccha", a nickname by which fans and the industry still refer to him today. This performance launched an extraordinary streak of critical and commercial success, leading to an unprecedented achievement: winning the Filmfare Award for Best Actor (Kannada) for three consecutive years for his roles in *Huchcha* (2001), *Nandhi* (2002), and *Swathi Muthu* (2003). His powerful performance in *Nandhi* also earned him the prestigious Karnataka State Film Award for Best Actor.

Expanding his creative footprint, Sudeep made a highly successful directorial debut with *My Autograph* (2006). He went on to direct and star in several major commercial blockbusters, including *Kempe Gowda* (2011) and *Maanikya* (2014). He catapulted to massive pan-Indian fame and widespread critical acclaim for his phenomenal performance as the ruthless, womanizing antagonist in S. S. Rajamouli's blockbuster Telugu-Tamil bilingual fantasy film *Eega* (2012), a role that won him the Filmfare Award for Best Supporting Actor – Telugu.
